## Mô tả

> In the early 1990s, Ron Harris was a computer expert at the Nevada Gaming Control Board, paid to find cheating software in casino games. He wrote the programs on the laptops inspectors plugged into slot machines, and hid something inside: machines he touched paid a jackpot when coins went in a set order. Then he went after keno. In January 1995 a $100,000 ticket at Bally's in Atlantic City brought it all down. So who checks the people who check the machines?
