## 2. Mô tả (YouTube)

> In May 1845, Sir John Franklin sailed from Greenhithe, near London, with two ships, Erebus and Terror, and food for three years. After five men were sent home from Greenland, 129 were left. Whalers saw the ships in Baffin Bay that summer; no European saw them again. They wintered at Beechey Island, where three men were buried, but left no note saying where they were going. In 1846 they took the west side of King William Island, where the ice doesn't always clear in summer, and on 12 September the ice closed around both ships. A note left on the shore in May 1847 said "All well." A year later, someone wrote around its margins: Franklin was dead, 24 men were dead, and the other 105 were leaving the ships to walk south. In 1854 Inuit told John Rae about men dragging a boat south, starving. London attacked the story, and the note was only found in 1859. Erebus was found in 2014, in waters where Inuit stories had long said a big ship sank; Terror in 2016, after an Inuk hunter remembered a mast in the ice. The wrong side of the island, no note at Beechey, or no one listening to the Inuit: what kept the ships lost for 169 years?
