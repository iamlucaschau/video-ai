> Italy was about two weeks' sail from Spain. Hannibal marched for five months instead, over the Alps, and lost about half his army on the passes. Rome had won the last war and ruled the sea, and the only road into Italy ran over the mountains, where Celtic tribes were ready to join him. Rome's fleet or the Celts beyond the Alps: which one decided his route?

> **Sources**
> - Polybius, *Histories* book 3: 3.11 (the oath at nine), 3.27 (Sicily and Sardinia), 3.34 (the Celts), 3.35 (troops left in Spain), 3.39 (distances), 3.41 (the Roman fleets), 3.42–46 (the Rhône, 37 elephants, the rafts), 3.49–55 (the Island, the climb, snow and the landslide), 3.56 (five months, fifteen days in the Alps, the survivors from Hannibal's own inscription at Lacinium), 3.60 (half the army lost in the passes)
> - Virgil, *Aeneid* 4.625 (Dido's avenger)
> - "Two weeks": our estimate. New Carthage → south of Sardinia → Tiber mouth ≈ 1,300 km by sea, at ~75–120 km per day of ancient sailing ≈ 11–17 days. "1,600 km": Polybius gives about 9,000 stades from New Carthage to the Po plain; at 8 stades to the Roman mile ≈ 1,665 km.
> - Historians still debate which pass Hannibal used. This map follows the Col de la Traversette route (de Beer 1955; Mahaney et al. 2016; PNAS 2026) and crosses the Rhône near Beaucaire–Tarascon. Other historians place the pass and the crossing differently.
