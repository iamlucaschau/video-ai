## 2. Mô tả (YouTube)

> In the 7th century, the monk Xuanzang left China's capital, Chang'an, to bring Buddhist texts back from India, about 2,500 km away in a straight line. The emperor had closed the borders, so he slipped out at night. He crossed the desert alone on one old horse, went around the Himalayas by the Silk Road, over the Tian Shan and the Hindu Kush, and then stayed in India for more than ten years. "I have passed seventeen years," he wrote to the emperor on the way home. He came back with 657 books on twenty horses, and nine centuries later his journey became the novel Journey to the West. The emperor's ban, the mountains, or the years he chose to stay: which one really made it seventeen years?

> - "About 2,500 km": straight-line distance from Chang'an (Xi'an) to Nalanda, our own calculation (2,479 km).
