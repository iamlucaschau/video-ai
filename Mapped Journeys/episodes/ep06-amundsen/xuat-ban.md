## 2. Mô tả (YouTube)

> Franklin's 129 men never came back. On a rainy night in June 1903, Roald Amundsen slipped out of Christiania (Oslo) with seven men, an old herring boat called Gjøa and food for five years, partly to get away from his creditors. In August they anchored at Beechey Island, "Franklin's last safe winter harbour", then sailed south down the same strait as Franklin. At King William Island Franklin had taken the west side; Amundsen took the east, through Rae Strait. In October 1903 the ice locked Gjøa into a small harbour, now Gjoa Haven, and Amundsen stayed almost two years. His men sledged out to locate the north magnetic pole, and the Netsilik Inuit next door taught them to dress in animal skins, build snow houses and travel with dogs, skills Franklin's naval crews had not been trained for. Gjøa left the harbour on 13 August 1905. Two weeks later came the shout "Vessel in sight, sir!": the whaler Charles Hanson, from San Francisco. They had made it through. The ice closed again off the Yukon coast, so Amundsen skied about 800 km to Eagle, Alaska, to send a telegram, and Gjøa reached Nome on 31 August 1906, three years after Oslo. In December 1911 he reached the South Pole first, in clothing modelled on the Netsilik's. Three years, and nearly two of them spent learning.
