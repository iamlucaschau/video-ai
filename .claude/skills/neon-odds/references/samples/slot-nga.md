Tiêu đề gốc: How the Russians predicted the "random" slot machine

This man's phone vibrates a quarter of a second before he presses spin. And that tiny buzz is worth ten thousand dollars a day.

In 2009, Russia banned almost all gambling overnight. Thousands of slot machines were sold off for almost nothing. Some were bought by engineers in St. Petersburg. Not to play them. To take them apart.

Inside, they found the machine's brain: a pseudorandom number generator. Pseudo. The numbers look random, but they come from a formula. Learn the formula, and the machine's future is an open book.

Here's how the attack works. An agent sits at a matching machine in a real casino. He records about two dozen spins on his phone and sends the video home. In St. Petersburg, computers match the results against the formula and calculate exactly where the machine sits in its endless sequence.

Then his phone starts sending timing signals. It vibrates 0.25 seconds before the perfect moment — because that's exactly how long a human finger needs to react. He doesn't decide when to spin. The phone does.

Individual agents were taking home more than ten thousand dollars a day. A four-person team could clear a quarter of a million dollars in a week.

Casinos could see the money leaving. They just couldn't see how. The only tell: a finger, hovering above the button, waiting for a signal nobody else could hear.

In December 2014, four agents were arrested in Missouri. But the man behind it stayed in St. Petersburg, out of reach. And here's the part that terrified casinos: these old machines can't be patched. The flaw is baked in. Replacing them would cost a fortune.

Then, in 2016, he emailed the manufacturer himself. The offer: he stops, and even helps fix their machines. The price: eight figures. They refused, and called law enforcement. So somewhere out there, the formula still exists. The only question is whose phone is buzzing right now.
