## VOICEOVER (English)

In 480 BC, the largest empire the world had yet seen marched on the small cities of Greece.

Its king, Xerxes, ruled from Egypt to the Indus. Ten years earlier, Athens had beaten his father's army at Marathon. Xerxes wanted revenge.

His army marched south along the coast, with his fleet beside it. To reach Athens, it had to pass one narrow gap between the mountains and the sea. Thermopylae.

Waiting there: about seven thousand Greeks. Their leader, the Spartan king Leonidas, had brought three hundred of his own men, all fathers with sons at home.

According to Plutarch, Xerxes told them to hand over their weapons. Leonidas replied: come and take them.

Herodotus claimed over two million men. Historians today think far fewer, but still many times the Greeks.

Xerxes waited four days for them to run. On the fifth, he attacked. For two days, his army could not break through. Not even his elite guard, the Immortals.

Then a local Greek, Ephialtes, showed the Persians a path over the mountain. Leonidas sent most of the Greeks away. He stayed with his three hundred and seven hundred men from Thespiae. Almost all of them died.

Xerxes marched on and burned Athens. But the victory did not last. Weeks later, his fleet was crushed at Salamis. The next year, his army was driven from Greece.

At the pass, a stone still carries their epitaph. Go tell the Spartans, passer-by, that here we lie, obedient to their laws.
