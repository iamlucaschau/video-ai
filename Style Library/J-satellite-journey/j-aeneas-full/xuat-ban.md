## 2. Mô tả (YouTube)

> Italy was about three weeks' sail from Troy. According to Virgil's Aeneid, it took Aeneas seven years. A misread oracle sent him to the wrong island, a prophet told him to avoid the Strait of Messina, and Juno, who loved Carthage, threw his fleet off course. Which stop cost him the most time?

> **Sources**
> - Virgil, *Aeneid*: 1.12–33 (Juno and Carthage), 1.755–756 and 5.626 (the seventh summer), books 2–3 (Troy to Drepanum), book 4 (Dido's curse, 4.622–629), book 6 (Cumae), 7.107–117 (eating the tables)
> - Livy, *Ab Urbe Condita* 1.1–7 (Aeneas, Alba Longa, Romulus and Remus); Encyclopaedia Britannica (Punic Wars, 264–146 BC)
> - "Three weeks": our estimate. Troy → Cape Malea → Strait of Messina → Tiber mouth ≈ 1,700 km by sea, at ~75–120 km per day of ancient sailing ≈ 14–22 days. "3,000+ km": sum of the straight legs between the 15 stops shown (≈ 3,170 km).
> - The route follows the order of the Aeneid. Some stops (Thrace, the Strophades) are placed differently by other traditions.
